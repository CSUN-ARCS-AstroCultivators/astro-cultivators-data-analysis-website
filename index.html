ASTRO CULTIVATORS

Early Prediction and Temporal Analysis of Growth and Stress for Soybean in Controlled Environments

About This Project

Astro Cultivators is a collaborative data analysis project at California State University, Northridge. The project is part of CSUN ARCS, the Autonomy Research Center for STEAHM. ARCS is a NASA sponsored Center of Excellence at CSUN, and its work connects student research with NASA, JPL, and industry partners.

Our team builds the imaging and analytics pipeline for early drought stress detection in soybean seedlings. The plants are grown in controlled chamber conditions, where drought stress is introduced by changing the watering schedule. Using hyperspectral, RGB, and depth imaging, we study plant growth and stress signals that may appear before they are obvious to the human eye.

The pipeline includes dataset organization, preprocessing, feature engineering, temporal growth analysis, and output visualization. The goal is to help biology researchers understand plant stress earlier and make the data easier to interpret.

Multi Sensor Data

Hyperspectral, RGB, and depth images are used to capture plant color, spectral response, area, and height.

Plant Stress Focus

The main research goal is to track drought stress and growth differences between control and drought plants.

Analysis Outputs

The notebooks generate masks, vegetation indices, feature tables, trend plots, and visualization summaries.

Project Context

Astro Cultivators is part of CSUN ARCS, a NASA sponsored Center of Excellence. Through ARCS, students work on research connected to autonomy, controlled environments, plant monitoring, and NASA/JPL inspired engineering and data analysis problems.
