Temporal Growth Analysis

This stage studies how plant features change over time so early stress patterns can be detected before they become visually obvious.

Temporal Analysis Goal

Instead of only looking at one image at a time, temporal growth analysis tracks plant measurements across days and image captures. This helps compare growth patterns between plant sets, identify data gaps, and detect possible stress signals earlier.

Hyperspectral Time Series

The hyperspectral pipeline keeps timestamp, date, and time with each feature row. This makes it possible to plot vegetation indices over time and compare spectral changes across the growth cycle.

NDVI Over Time

NDVI trends show how plant greenness and vegetation response change during the imaging period.

GNDVI Over Time

GNDVI trends help track chlorophyll related response using green and near infrared wavelengths.

Coverage Over Time

Coverage estimates how much plant region appears in each frame, which can reflect visible growth or segmentation quality.

Data Gaps and Set Selection

The RealSense workflow checked how many matched RGB and depth pairs were available per day and searched for missing dates. This matters because temporal analysis depends on consistent captures across the plant cycle.

  • RGB and depth images were matched by timestamp.
  • Images outside the main grow light window were removed.
  • Matched pairs were counted by day.
  • Set 1 and Set 2 were separated by date.
  • Set 2 was chosen for cleaner feature extraction.

Stress Pattern Interpretation

The main purpose of temporal analysis is to look for differences between healthy growth and stress affected growth. A stress signal may appear as a drop or shift in vegetation indices, slower area growth, lower visible greenness, or abnormal height trends.

The current notebooks show temporal feature extraction and plotting. Final biological interpretation should be reviewed with the biology team before being treated as a final research conclusion.
